In short. We use a small number of strictly necessary cookies and local-storage entries to keep you signed in, secure the site and remember your cookie choice. Analytics, preferences and marketing technologies are switched off until you turn them on in the EEA, the UK, Switzerland, Brazil and India, and are off until you opt out in the US states that require a universal opt-out. We honour the Global Privacy Control (GPC) signal. We do not load advertising or cross-site tracking pixels.

1. What cookies and similar technologies are

1.1 Cookies are small text files a site stores on your device. Local storage (also called web storage) keeps larger key-value data in your browser. Pixels and SDKs are small code fragments or app libraries that report interactions. We use "cookies" in this policy to mean all of them.

1.2 First-party cookies are set by us. Third-party cookies are set by another domain — for example a font or analytics provider.

1.3 Session cookies are deleted when you close your browser. Persistent cookies remain until they expire or you delete them.

1.4 Similar technologies we may use: HTML5 local storage and session storage, IndexedDB, browser fingerprinting resistance checks, and — in apps — device identifiers and SDKs.

1.5 What cookies are not. Cookies do not carry viruses and cannot read files on your device. They are not malware.

Withdrawing consent is always as easy as giving it. Withdrawal is not retroactive but stops all further processing.

3.2 Global Privacy Control (GPC)

We detect and honour the GPC signal as a valid opt-out. When your browser sends it, we treat it exactly as if you had submitted an opt-out request — across cookies, pixels and third-party integrations.

Twelve states now require GPC recognition: California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Maryland, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Oregon and Texas. Firefox and Brave send GPC by default.

3.3 Browser and device controls

You can also block or delete cookies in your browser settings — Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Opera all offer this, usually under Settings → Privacy and Security. Blocking strictly necessary cookies will break sign-in and may stop the Service working.

Useful resources: aboutcookies.org · allaboutcookies.org

3.4 Do Not Track

There is still no common standard for DNT. We therefore honour GPC (a defined standard) rather than DNT, but we treat a DNT signal as an opt-out request too.

3.5 Mobile

On iOS and Android you can reset or limit the advertising identifier, and use "Ask App Not to Track". Our apps request the App Tracking Transparency permission where required before any tracking.
